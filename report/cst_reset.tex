\section{Experiment 1: a new CST database}
\textbf{Question.} Can two or three coordinates preserve a selected aerodynamic
quantity while supporting a useful map back to realizable airfoils and fields?
This experiment implements \texttt{next\_experimentations.md}. The previous
NACA population is historical context; none of its fields or fitted reductions
enter this study.

Standard Class--Shape Transformation (CST) \cite{kulfan2008} defines the two
surfaces, in chord units, by
\[
 y^{u,l}(x)=x^{1/2}(1-x)\sum_{j=0}^{7}a^{u,l}_j
 \binom{7}{j}x^j(1-x)^{7-j}\ \mathbin{\pm}\ \frac{0.0025}{2}x,
 \quad x\in[0,1].
\]
There is no leading-edge modification. Sample half-thickness weights
$h_j\in[0.10,0.30]$ and camber weights $b_j\in[-0.06,0.10]$, with $b_0=0$,
and set $a^u=h+b$, $a^l=-h+b$. This gives 15 independent variables, equal
upper/lower nose radii, and positive thickness. Degree-seven Bernstein
polynomials and the fixed class function define the geometry directly; this
is not a fit to a NACA family. Ordinate derivatives diverge at the rounded
nose \cite{kulfan2008}, so the proposed $C^1([0,1])$ graph assumption must be
replaced by regularity on the interior or a regular contour parameterization.

A scrambled Sobol pool of 2,048 proposals (seed 42) provides 768 samples after
a 301-station geometric screen: maximum thickness in $[0.08,0.20]$ and
maximum absolute camber at most 0.05. The sampled thickness range is
0.101--0.200 and camber magnitude range 0.0011--0.0309. The centered
coefficient matrix has rank 15.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering\includegraphics[width=.94\linewidth]{dataset.pdf}
\caption{Six shapes selected by equally spaced thickness ranks, and their
predicted drag curves at the largest Reynolds number. These illustrate the
new CST corpus rather than physical class labels.}
\end{figure}

NeuralFoil 0.3.3, model size \texttt{large}, evaluates $C_D,C_L,C_M$ and
pointwise analysis confidence on $31\times21$ conditions:
$\alpha\in[-5,12]^\circ$, $Re\in[3\times10^5,3\times10^6]$, geometrically
spaced in Reynolds number. Mach is zero, $n_{\rm crit}=9$, and transition is
unforced. The corpus contains 499,968 aerodynamic cases. All finite positive-drag
outputs are retained, including low-confidence cases. The primary analysis
requires minimum confidence across the domain at least 0.75: 598 shapes
qualify and 170 are excluded from analysis, not deleted. Confidence is a model
diagnostic, not a validated error bound \cite{neuralfoil}.

The fixed random split is 388 training, 89 validation, and 121 test shapes.
The default objective is literally
\[
 q(F)=\min_{(\alpha,Re)\in\Omega_{\rm grid}} C_D(\alpha,Re).
\]
It is a grid minimum, without prescribed lift or trim. In 87.1\% of eligible
shapes the minimum occurs at the largest Reynolds number; it is not a measure
of good performance throughout the domain. The code also supports mean drag
and negative mean absolute-lift/drag objectives.

\section{Experiment 2: preserving a quantity versus preserving fields}
Use the normalized product measure $d\alpha\,d\log Re$ and training-only
coefficient scales $s_c$. The common balanced field distance is
\[
 d_F(i,j)^2=\sum_{c\in\{D,L,M\}}s_c^{-2}
 \left\langle(F_{ic}-F_{jc})^2\right\rangle_\Omega.
\]
Functional PCA (FPCA) diagonalizes the quadrature-weighted training fields
\cite{happ2016}. Ordinary functional diffusion maps (FDM) provide a nonlinear
control \cite{barroso2024}. The quantity-aware variant is this experiment's
explicit kernel choice, not a claim attributed to that paper:
\[
 D_{ij}^2=\frac{d_F(i,j)^2}{\operatorname{median}_{i<j}d_F(i,j)^2}
       +\lambda\frac{(q_i-q_j)^2}{s_q^2},\qquad
 K_{ij}=\exp(-D_{ij}^2/\varepsilon).
\]
Normalize kernel density with exponent one, row-normalize to a Markov
matrix, and use its nonconstant right eigenvectors multiplied by their
eigenvalues (diffusion time one). Held-out fields use Nystr\"om extension.
Bandwidth multipliers are $\{0.1,0.25,1,4,16\}$ times the median training
$D^2$, and quantity weights are $\{0.1,0.25,1,4,16\}$. Ordinary FDM uses
$\lambda=0$. Validation selects ordinary FDM by field reconstruction and
quantity-aware FDM by quantity-neighbor error. No test row is used in fitting
scales, bases, kernel bandwidths, or surrogate parameters.

\textbf{Information supplied.} Every encoder receives the aerodynamic field.
Quantity-aware extension also computes $q$ from that field. Thus the test
scores below measure preservation after projection of known fields, not blind
prediction from new CST coefficients. A scalar coordinate $z=q$ would preserve
$q$ exactly by definition; small quantity error alone cannot establish a useful
field inverse or a new aerodynamic predictor.

QoI NRMSE is the held-out error of a smooth RBF kernel-ridge fit divided by
training $s_q$; $\rho$ is its Spearman rank correlation. Field error is the
weighted squared error of ten-neighbor field reconstruction divided by
training-mean prediction error. Field and shape recall measure recovery of
five training neighbors for each test query. Chance recall is 1.29\%.
The same neighbor decoder is used for every method.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering\includegraphics[width=.94\linewidth]{metric_tradeoff.pdf}
\caption{Validation diagnostics for the full kernel search. Each point is a
bandwidth/quantity-weight choice; colors encode quantity weight. Lower errors
are preferable. Scalar preservation and full-field retention compete.}
\end{figure}

Two and three FPCA coordinates retain 68.1\% and 77.2\% of training variance;
26 are needed for 99\%. Their direct linear test reconstruction errors are
0.287 and 0.206. Quantity-aware FDM gives conditional QoI NRMSE
0.0047--0.0054 but field error above 1.24, worse than the training mean.
Choosing a weight solely for scalar preservation sacrifices much of the field.

\section{Experiment 3: reading the projections and their clusters}
\begin{figure}[h]
\centering\includegraphics[width=.90\linewidth]{projection_maps.pdf}
\caption{All 598 eligible shapes projected with training-fitted maps. Columns
compare FPCA, ordinary FDM, and quantity-aware FDM in two dimensions. Rows
color the same coordinates by minimum drag, maximum thickness, and signed
camber at the largest absolute-camber station. Each row has a common color
scale; coordinate scales differ between methods.}
\end{figure}

The quantity-aware map forms a thin, ordered curve: nearby coordinates have
similar $q$, while thickness and camber still vary along that curve. Its
ten-neighbor quantity spread is 0.046 training standard deviations, compared
with 0.841 for FPCA-2. This compression of scalar variation does not preserve
shape or aerodynamic neighborhoods. Quantity-aware five-neighbor recalls
are 3.3\% for fields and 3.5\% for shapes; FPCA-3 recovers 43.3\% and 7.4\%.
The three-dimensional maps are saved separately and do not resolve the
quantity-aware field-reconstruction failure.

Four-cluster K-means fits (20 initializations, seed 42) use training coordinates
and assign all eligible shapes. FPCA-2 cluster mean $q$ values overlap strongly,
from 0.00550 to 0.00630. Quantity-aware cluster means span 0.00488--0.00741;
this is expected from the chosen scalar similarity, not independent evidence
of physical airfoil classes. Cluster labels and member summaries are persisted.

Ordinary FDM-2 has a localized spectral mode: the smallest coordinate
participation fraction is 0.011, equivalent to concentration on roughly four
of 388 training points. Its four-cluster population counts are 588, 1, 8, 1.
The selected FDM-3 bandwidth is broader and participation rises to 0.245.
Narrow-band pilot results are preserved rather than removed. Outlier
localization is an observed limitation of this kernel/data pairing, not proof
of a general disadvantage of functional diffusion maps.

\section{Experiment 4: optimization and inverse-map consistency}
For each map, fit an RBF kernel-ridge scalar surrogate using only training
coordinates. Validation chooses ridge $\{0.001,0.01,0.1\}$ and length-scale
multiplier $\{0.5,1,2\}$. Eight SLSQP starts minimize it inside the standardized
training convex hull. A declared $10^{-6}$ interior buffer resolves boundary
roundoff. A Delaunay barycentric decoder interpolates CST coefficients;
convexity preserves coefficient bounds and positive thickness. Geometry is
rechecked on 1,001 stations, and every reported candidate receives a fresh
NeuralFoil evaluation. Up to three distinct decoded shapes per method are
verified; coefficient distances below $10^{-4}$ are treated as duplicates.
Failed solves and all candidate outputs are retained.

For a field generator $G$, encoder $E$, and CST decoder $D$, define
\[
 \delta(z)=\frac{\|E(G(D(z)))-z\|}
 {\operatorname{median}_{i<j}\|z_i-z_j\|}.
\]
The convex hull is an optimization support, not a demonstrated image
$A=E(G(\mathcal P))$. A geometrically valid decoder can move the generated
field away from the coordinate optimized by the surrogate.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering\includegraphics[width=.94\linewidth]{optimization.pdf}
\caption{Surrogate predictions and fresh aerodynamic evaluations of all
verified candidates. The dashed line is the best training shape. Smoothness

Quantity-aware candidates improve \texttt{large}-model grid drag by about
2.6\%, but have round-trip discrepancies 0.16--0.27 of typical pair distance.
Re-evaluation on a $61\times41$ grid reduces the gain to about 1.4\% against
the finer-grid incumbent. The 12 fixed training-grid checks change $q$ by at
most 1.21\%. Comparing \texttt{large} with \texttt{xxxlarge} across 18 probes
changes $q$ by up to 7.55\%; under \texttt{xxxlarge}, quantity-aware candidates
are about 1.1--1.2\% worse than the original incumbent. This is solver-size
sensitivity within NeuralFoil, not validation against independent physics.

\textbf{Answer and next test.} Quantity-driven scalar preservation is possible
and empirically strong here, but a reliable, easily optimized full-field inverse
is not established. Next, jointly constrain field loss and round-trip error,
and use a prescribed-lift mean-drag objective with independent aerodynamic
validation. Minimum-over-domain objectives can be nonsmooth when the active
operating point changes; CST smoothness on the interior does not remove this.

\section*{Reproduction and evidence}
The active database is in \texttt{data/cst\_reset/}. Its README documents the
array schema; the raw cache retains all coefficient vectors, fields, confidence,
axes and generation settings. The catalogue provides one row per shape.

Run from the repository root:
\begin{verbatim}
python notebooks/cst_reset.py
python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
\end{verbatim}
\texttt{notebooks/cst\_reset.ipynb} runs the same script and displays the
evidence. Existing project numerical dependencies suffice. Versions, source
and cache hashes, split indices, seed, kernels and surrogate parameters are
recorded. Numerical coordinates and minima can vary across library versions.

Change the objective or field similarity without changing dataset generation:
\begin{verbatim}
python notebooks/cst_reset.py --qoi mean_drag
python notebooks/cst_reset.py --metric drag
\end{verbatim}
Alternative runs have separate directories and tables. This PDF describes
\texttt{min\_drag} with balanced $C_D,C_L,C_M$ similarity. Reusable methods are
in \texttt{utils/cst\_reduction.py}; orchestration stays with the experiment.

From \texttt{report/}, build the default report:
\begin{verbatim}
pdflatex -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error cst_reset.tex
bibtex cst_reset
pdflatex -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error cst_reset.tex
pdflatex -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error cst_reset.tex
\end{verbatim}

\textbf{Evidence.} Results are in \path{data/cst_reset/min_drag_balanced/}.
The principal tables are \path{projection_metrics.csv} for preservation and
\path{optimization.csv} for candidate verification. Companion tables record
validation searches, assignments, grid checks, model-size checks, and adopted
designs. Metadata records settings and hashes. Compressed NumPy files preserve
fitted arrays, decoded coefficients, candidate fields, and all solve statuses.
Pilot evidence records the narrower initial bandwidth search. All seven figure
sets are generated by code.

The result is exploratory: a single scrambled design and split, a declared
confidence-conditioned cohort, a finite operating grid, and surrogate-only
aerodynamics. There is no universal manifold, unique decoder, guaranteed
global optimum, geometry-to-latent predictor, CFD validation, or experiment-based
accuracy claim. The observed field and round-trip failures are part of the
result and are retained in the evidence.
